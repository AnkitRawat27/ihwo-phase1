Victim rights

General information to help people think about safety, evidence, privacy and access to qualified support.

KNOW YOUR OPTIONS

Your safety and dignity matter.

Victim rights and legal procedures differ by country and case type. The information below is general and is not legal advice.

Safety first

If you are in immediate danger, move to a safer place if possible and contact the local emergency number or a trusted local service.

Be informed

You can ask a qualified local lawyer or victim-support organisation about reporting options, confidentiality, protection and available remedies.

Protect your privacy

Share sensitive details only with trusted, appropriate professionals. Ask how your information will be stored and used.

Keeping information safely

  • Keep original messages, emails, receipts and documents where lawful and safe.
  • Record dates, times, locations and a factual sequence of events.
  • Do not edit, fabricate, delete or publicly post evidence to pressure anyone.
  • Do not access another person's account or device without permission.
  • Make a secure backup and share copies only with appropriate professionals or authorities.

Before making a report

Ask about the process, possible risks, confidentiality limits, support person availability and what may happen after a report. A lawyer or specialist victim-support organisation can explain local rules.

Important: IHWO does not assess guilt or decide whether a crime occurred. That is for competent authorities and courts under applicable law.
