VICTIM RIGHTS

Your safety and dignity matter.

General information to help people consider safety, privacy, documentation and access to qualified support. Rights and procedures differ by country.

Not legal advice: This page is general information only. A qualified local lawyer or victim-support service can explain the rules that apply to your situation.

Safety first

If you are in immediate danger, move to a safer place if possible and contact local emergency services or a trusted local support service.

Be informed

Ask a qualified professional about reporting options, confidentiality, protection measures, time limits and available remedies.

Protect your privacy

Share sensitive details only with trusted and appropriate professionals. Ask how your information will be stored, used and shared.

Preserving information safely

  • Keep original messages, emails, receipts and documents where lawful and safe.
  • Write a factual timeline with dates, times, locations and what you personally observed.
  • Do not edit, fabricate, destroy or publicly post evidence to pressure anyone.
  • Do not access another person's account, device or private records without permission or lawful authority.
  • Keep secure backups and share copies only with appropriate professionals or authorities.
  • Do not put yourself at risk to collect additional evidence.

Before making a report

You may wish to ask what the reporting process involves, what confidentiality limits apply, whether a support person can accompany you and what may happen after a report. A local lawyer or specialist victim-support organisation can explain the relevant procedures.

IHWO does not decide whether a crime occurred or whether a person is guilty. Those decisions belong to competent authorities and courts under applicable law.
